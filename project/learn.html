Learn the Art of Drifting

1. What is Drifting?

Drifting is a motorsport technique where the driver intentionally oversteers, causing a loss of traction in the rear wheels while maintaining vehicle control through the entire corner entry, apex, and exit.

2. Weight Transfer Mechanics

Initiating a drift requires shifting the car's weight forward onto the front tires to unload the rear tires. Flicking the steering wheel while tapping the brakes or clutch (the "Feint Technique") unseats rear traction cleanly.

3. Counter-Steering & Throttle Control

Once the rear steps out, rapidly counter-steer into the direction of the slide. Modulate the throttle precisely: too much power causes a spin-out, while releasing the gas completely instantly straightens the vehicle.

4. Essential Safety Equipment

Safety is paramount. Professional drifting requires a certified roll cage, 5-point harness, Snell-rated helmet, fire-retardant suit, and a handheld fire extinguisher mounted in the cabin.

Beginner Tips

  • ✔ Practice on closed tracks or skid pads.
  • ✔ Start in a low-power, rear-wheel-drive (RWD) vehicle.
  • ✔ Weld or install a Limited Slip Differential (LSD).
  • ✔ Keep tire pressure high in the rear for learning.
